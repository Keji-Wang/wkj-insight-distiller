# Livestream / Podcast / Subtitle Preprocess

Use this file when the input is a livestream transcript, podcast subtitle file, panel discussion, or two-person dialogue with heavy noise.

## Goal

Do not summarize the transcript line by line.
First turn noisy speech into a stable structure that can support judgment.

## Step 1: Remove low-value noise

Delete or compress these unless they change the argument:

- greetings and sign-offs
- thanks, gifts, memberships, donations, likes, follows
- platform operation talk
- mic, lag, connection, weather, scheduling chatter
- repeated catchphrases and repeated emotional emphasis

If a platform version is longer only because it includes post-show chatter, mark that difference clearly instead of over-interpreting it.

## Step 2: Rebuild the speaking structure

Identify:

- who is mainly explaining
- who is mainly prompting
- where the topic changes
- where the speaker shifts from fact to interpretation

For each segment, write a short label such as:

- event claim
- policy interpretation
- macro framework
- emotional mobilization
- joke / rhetoric

## Step 3: Separate source layers

For each important statement, decide which layer it belongs to:

1. **Source-mentioned fact**
- something the speaker says happened
- something cited from a report, article, video, or rumor

2. **Interpretive view**
- the speaker's explanation of what the fact means

3. **Framework claim**
- a broader model used to connect many events

4. **Emotional rhetoric**
- ridicule, sarcasm, applause lines, identity signaling

Do not treat layer 1 as externally verified fact by default.

## Step 4: Reconstruct argument chains

For each major theme, map:

- claim
- supporting evidence mentioned in the source
- missing evidence
- leap in logic

If the transcript is clipped or interrupted, mark `logic interrupted`.
If the speaker relies on rumors, unverified screenshots, or "someone said", mark `verification required`.

## Step 5: Decide the material type

Choose the dominant type:

- **fact-heavy material**: events, dates, policies, institutional moves
- **framework-heavy material**: worldview, ideological lens, macro explanation
- **mixed material**: both factual claims and framing logic are important

Why this matters:

- fact-heavy material usually needs Research-Enhanced follow-up
- framework-heavy material often has value even before external verification
- mixed material should be split before synthesis

## Step 6: Compress before synthesis

Do not carry the whole transcript into the memo.
Compress it into:

- 3-5 main themes
- 3-5 high-value signals
- 1-3 reusable judgments
- 2-3 expressions worth saving only if they are transferable

## Step 7: Business translation

After extracting the themes, ask:

- does this reveal a demand-side signal?
- does this reveal a policy implementation clue?
- does this reveal a talent, market, or product timing issue?
- is this useful for customer conversation, public sharing, or internal knowledge?

If not, keep it as source summary only and do not over-upgrade it into an "insight".
